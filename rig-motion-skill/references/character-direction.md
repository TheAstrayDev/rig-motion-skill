# Character → decision → movement

Search: personality, acting, intention, temperament, motivation, character.

## Establish character before new acting

Character is a practical directing tool. Establish **how they behave, what they want now, and what they do when it becomes difficult**. Names, skins, and colors do not answer this. Use the [character card](../assets/character-card.md), reusing information from the prompt or project.

If information is missing or contradictory enough to change acting, ask one compact question: “How do A and B behave: calm and precise, bold and impulsive, or cautious and cunning? What does each want here?” Offer relevant choices and allow a free answer. While waiting, inspect sources, record sketch observations, and prepare neutral layout. Do not bake a final acting interpretation before the necessary answer.

“I do not know what they should do” with known personalities delegates the story; invent it. “Choose the personalities too” delegates character; state your chosen traits and intention, then proceed. An unanswered question does not provide that delegation. Narrow repairs to sliding, export, or camera noise do not need a new personality interview.

## Make the card affect motion

| Field | Practical consequence |
|---|---|
| Two or three traits and an internal contradiction | Repeated decisions and a visible vulnerability |
| Present goal and stakes | Attention, attempted change, and what counts as winning |
| Strategy | Persuade, avoid, pressure, deceive, endure, improvise |
| Response to error/danger | Retreat, anger, analysis, humor, concealed weakness |
| Physical profile | Mass, agility, experience, fatigue, real rig limitations |
| Relationship | Distance, expected response, provocation, care, rivalry |
| Final change | Confidence, control, trust, status, or understanding |

Do not infer physique automatically from temperament: calm can be light; nervous can be heavy. Angry is not a direction to wave the entire body. Choose a concrete strategy.

## Translate traits into visible choices

This is an author directing model, not a psychological test or a compulsory stereotype.

| Profile | Preparation and attention | Action | Reaction and stop |
|---|---|---|---|
| Calm, precise | Small weight shift; gaze selects the target early | Short clean path, stable base | Regains control quickly and checks the result |
| Impulsive, confident | Reveals intention early; large preparation | Abrupt entry, excessive effort | Overshoots, searches for support, saves face |
| Cautious, cunning | Tests distance, false start, observation | Economical bypass/counter, purposeful redirection | Stays ready; retreat serves a goal |
| Heavy, stubborn | Builds effort slowly; resists changing course | Strong push with stable mass | Longer braking and delayed reactions through the chain |
| Light, playful | Tests/provokes; pauses before a surprise | Quick changes with genuine support transfers | Lively settling and partner assessment, without constant bounce |
| Afraid, determined | Retreat and goal compete in the pose | A risky burst after deciding | A breath/realization pause, then another choice |

Choose two or three differences readable in a rigid silhouette: preparation duration, torso path, distance, head turns, confidence of support, stop quality. Jitter cannot replace a decision. Use R6's whole parts; do not invent brows, fingers, or joints. Use only controls that actually exist on a Minecraft cinematic rig.

## When the user delegates the story

1. Express the goal with a verb: hold, unbalance, prove, protect, escape.
2. Choose an obstacle that provokes a specific response from this personality.
3. Build a short arc: observe → decide → attempt → partner response → change → final reaction.
4. Make mechanics follow character. Precise A manages distance and catches an error; impulsive A overcommits; caring A reduces force once the goal is reached.
5. Give B a goal and initiative. B responds to perceived action and physical force rather than serving as an effects target.
6. Preserve actions and outcomes explicitly requested in the sketch/prompt. Invent connecting steps, pauses, and endings within the delegation.

The same “A lifts a leg → B falls” can be a precise counter, a forceful attack, or a comic accident. Choose through user-defined personality, intention, and visible evidence. Stick-figure color does not establish force or morality.

## Acting pass

Animate thought first: attention target → perceived change → decision → action → assessment. B may anticipate a visible threat before contact; physical knockback needs contact or another established force. Verify the time of each cause.

Watch without VFX/SFX. Could the viewer distinguish A and B with identical skins? Does the outcome change behavior? Does every character use the same heroic settle? If personality disappears, revise the decisive pose and action choice before adding details. Record confirmed information and author choices separately.

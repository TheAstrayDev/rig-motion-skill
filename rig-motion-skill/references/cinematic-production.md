# One request to a finished animation

Search: pipeline, production, single prompt, autonomous, animatic, staging, sequence.

Use this workflow for a multi-beat film, especially from a sketch. For a small repair, use only affected stages. When usable tools are available, execute production; a written plan is not a completed animation.

## 1. Input and technical contract

Establish product, rig, style, limits, FPS/range, duration, and files from supplied information. If duration is delegated, choose it from readable beats and state the choice. Interpret the reference through [sketch analysis](sketch-to-shot.md); retain actor IDs and facts/hypotheses. Establish [character](character-direction.md). Derive a delegated story from known personality.

Inspect the actual project and save a working copy before structural changes. Check dependencies and available tools. For gameplay, make an early export/trigger probe; for film, test rig extremes, skin, shading, and one render. Avoid committing a large production to an unconfirmed technical route.

## 2. Directing statement

Write one sentence: “A wants X, attempts Y in their characteristic way, B answers Z, and W changes.” Build causal beats with a leading actor, attention target, pose, contact, and duration. Invent connecting phases within the delegated idea.

Choose the scene's primary accent. Give other events lower visual/audio amplitude. Contrast, clear mass, and consequences create force; every gesture need not peak at maximum intensity.

## 3. Layout and animatic

Place floor, proxies/actors, essential props, and camera. Solve distance, interaction axis, movement direction, and contact locations first. Use three to six decisive poses per shot with DOF, blur, shake, and VFX disabled. Check rigid-limb reach.

Build a real stepped preview with provisional holds. For multiple shots, check editing and screen direction. Fix unmotivated cuts, hidden support feet, obscured strikes, and a missing partner before detailed curves. Do not change the entire camera angle for every pose without a story reason.

Gate: normal-speed playback explains action and final reaction, actors remain continuous, and important extremes/support are achievable. A contact sheet is not a playback preview.

## 4. Primary mechanics

Work root/torso → support → leading limb → head/remaining limbs. Establish contact windows, force preparation, release, reaction, and absorption. Animate interacting actors together so A's path explains B's response. Use actual IK/FK controls within the R6/Minecraft limits.

Inspect neutral front, side, three-quarter, and final camera views. A cinematic cheat still needs visible causality. Gameplay clips must work from real game views and with engine-controlled trajectories.

## 5. Timing, spacing, and acting polish

Add breakdowns and duration contrast. Acceleration reads through spacing; stopping reads through force absorption. Do not automatically smooth contact holds. Add two or three chosen behavioral differences. Repair feet, grips, contacts, and flips before secondary action.

Keep a pass without camera embellishment or effects. If force is unclear there, repair movement. Significant retiming updates the [event sheet](../assets/event-sheet.md). Camera, VFX, and audio cues share marker IDs and a timeline origin.

## 6. Camera and light

Refine the base path and focus through [camera direction](camera-direction.md). Recheck silhouettes and contacts. Put motivated following, recoil, whips, and shake on appropriate separate controls; avoid tracking that cancels shake or creates target feedback.

Lighting separates actors from background and reveals form/speed. Check skin colors, pixel textures, and value hierarchy. Judge DOF and blur against already verified motion. Preview the camera without decorative effects, then reconsider effect scale after reframing.

## 7. Original Blender VFX

Create an effect collection and controls using [VFX guidance](vfx-blender.md). Establish graphic silhouette and lifetime, then materials, instances/simulation, and compositing. Controlled meshes, curves, or Grease Pencil can serve simple stylized shots. Complexity needs a visible purpose.

Fix seeds and birth causes. Save/bake stateful caches and test nonconsecutive frames after reopening. Render before, at, and after the main contact plus a continuous fragment. Inspect opacity, face overlap, blur, and flicker. Reduce an effect if it takes attention from the gesture.

## 8. SFX and mix

Spot events through [sound design](sfx-sound-design.md). Select and listen to real samples/foley and complete the source ledger. Place the main transient at contact, whoosh along the drive, and debris at secondary contacts. Perspective and contrast follow framing/character. Build causal sound before sweeteners and optional music.

Save stems and mix. Check rendered audio with rendered video; retiming requires another synchronization pass. Missing or muted media is not a completed SFX pass. Source rights determine whether raw samples may travel with an editable project.

## 9. Reviews and repair

Use [quality review](quality-review.md): undecorated movement; camera/VFX without sound; sound with image; final normal-speed mix. Record frame, cause, and repair for visible problems. Fix intention/contact first, visibility second, effects/mix third. Repeat affected previews after changes; an entire final render is unnecessary to test one node.

Pass count does not guarantee quality. Acceptance follows observable criteria. A remaining material defect means the scene is not ready.

## 10. Save and deliver

Save native source, controls/actions, event sheet, source ledger, relative media paths, and required caches. Reopen a copy and check texture/audio dependencies. Deliver final preview/mix, a mechanics-review version, and engine export/runtime evidence for game work. Name only files actually created.

If a tool is unavailable, continue independent useful work: reference breakdown, character/beat sheet, cue data, and implementable recipes. Report the specific blocker and completed work. Do not replace a real Blender render with an unrelated GIF or still. Instructions alone do not guarantee a masterpiece; execution and review determine the result.
